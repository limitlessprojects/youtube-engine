## Fact sheet — verified 2026-11-08 (DO NOT weaken or inflate)

- **The myth is documented as a misconception**: Royal Observatory Greenwich, "Why
  is the sky blue?" — "It's a common misconception that the sky is blue because it
  reflects the blue of the seas and oceans. In fact, it's the Earth's atmosphere…"
  Phrase: "The famous answer: oceans" + "It's wrong."
- **Sky blue = Rayleigh scattering off air molecules; blue scattered ~6× harder
  than red**: (700/450)⁴ = 5.86 (computed day-009; Wikipedia "Rayleigh scattering",
  HyperPhysics "Blue Sky and Rayleigh Scattering"). Same number as #0003 — do NOT
  say "ten times".
- **Deeper blue away from the sun**: HyperPhysics blusky.html — "the blue of the sky
  is more saturated when you look further from the sun" (Mie glare near the sun
  washes it out). Phrase: "Aim your eyes away from the sun — deepest blue."
- **Sahara framing**: logical entailment of the mechanism (the sky's blue comes from
  air, not the ocean — so inland skies are equally blue); RMG states the cause is
  the atmosphere. "A thousand kilometers" is ordinary-language scale of the Sahara's
  interior distance from the Atlantic/Mediterranean — keep it as scale, not a
  measured claim.
- **Ocean's blue is intrinsic — water absorbs red preferentially, blue remains and
  back-scatters**: Library of Congress "Everyday Mysteries: Why is the ocean blue?",
  Ethan Siegel "The sky is blue. The ocean is, too…" (blue light penetrates deepest;
  water preferentially absorbs red). Phrase: "Water eats red, keeps blue."
- **Do NOT claim**: "the ocean reflects nothing" (false at grazing angles — surface
  reflection is real); "the ocean copies the sky" (the script's phrasing is the
  NEGATION: it ISN'T copying — intrinsic absorption is the reason); any violet
  sensitivity claims (out of scope).
